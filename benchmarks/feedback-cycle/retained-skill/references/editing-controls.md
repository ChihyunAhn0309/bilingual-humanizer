# Turn an editorial brief into decisions

Use for substantial rewrites or voice matching. These controls refine the existing composition method; they do not add another mandatory draft, require external services, or change the iteration budget.

## Establish the target from evidence

Separate **reading knowledge**, **register**, and **rewrite scope**. An expert can want plain language without losing technical distinctions; a friendly notice can still contain binding instructions. Simplifying vocabulary must not simplify the claim. Infer these choices from the source and request. Use the user's terminology for a style request rather than pretending a commercial product mode is installed.

Identify a few distinctive voice cues that the source or authorized sample actually supports. Useful cues concern how the writer addresses the reader, handles uncertainty, moves between observations, or expresses a judgment. Link each chosen cue to a span. A reflective source might move from doubt to a specific observation; a work update might separate an apology from an action. Neither licenses adding a personal anecdote. With little voice evidence, prefer a suitable genre/register and leave personality unspecified. Do not manufacture three or four traits to fill a quota.

Track protected material separately from changeable prose. User-frozen terms, exact quotations, numbers, names, citations and commands remain fixed. A commercial tool's bracket or markup syntax is not a convention to impose on a user's text. Only treat a marker as an instruction when the user defines it; otherwise preserve literal punctuation.

## Choose the unit that needs work

Use a structural rewrite when information order, duplicated functions or dispersed qualifications burden the reader. Use a clause or sentence edit when the structure already works. Keep an effective passage when changing it offers no clear benefit. This decision is local: a document can need a new opening and only small edits elsewhere.

For a stubborn passage, consider two genuinely different ways to express its relationship, rather than a chain of synonym substitutions. Compare:

- whether the actor, speech act, scope and force survive;
- whether the next sentence connects without an invented bridge;
- whether the wording fits the established voice and knowledge level.

Select one and read the surrounding paragraph. Do not combine attractive fragments from incompatible alternatives or deliver several versions unless requested. Candidate comparison uses the existing revision allowance; there is no automatic best-of-N detector search.

English example, synthesized for this skill:

> I liked the first version, but the new caption makes the date easier to find. I still miss the old photograph.

Making this sound professional does not justify `The new version is better`. That loses both the reason and the mixed judgment. A restrained edit may keep most of the source.

한국어 예시:

> 안내문은 전보다 읽기 쉬워졌다. 그래도 신청 마감 시간이 맨 아래에 있어 한 번 더 찾았다.

`안내문이 개선되었으나 정보 접근성에는 한계가 있다`는 구체적인 경험과 말투를 일반적인 평가로 바꾼다. `안내문은 전보다 읽기 쉬워졌지만, 맨 아래에 있는 신청 마감 시간은 한 번 더 찾아야 했다`처럼 두 판단을 연결할 수 있다. 실제 경험이 원문에 없으면 새로 만들지 않는다.

## Review the effect, not the amount changed

Compare the draft with the source-backed cues and the proposition ledger in separate passes. Does a candid request now sound like a policy memo? Did a technical report become casual? Has an explanation become a bare assertion? Repair that specific drift, then check against the original again.

For detector experiments, freeze a complete faithful candidate before submitting it. A local alternative is not externally validated merely because a different draft scored well. Preserve exact checkpoints and compare native service metrics separately. Measured improvement on a selected document is not evidence of general improvement across languages or genres.
