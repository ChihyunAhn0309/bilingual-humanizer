# Compose natural English from the meaning

Read for substantial English rewriting after the shared meaning map and editorial brief. This refines sentence composition and review within the existing revision budget; it is not an extra mandatory draft. Use the source for facts and the brief for delivery. A passage that already works can stay.

## Choose how the writer speaks to this reader

Keep register distinct from stiffness. A professional reply can use ordinary verbs and contractions; the absence of contractions in a draft does not establish an authorial ban. Infer permission from genre, relationship and any explicit style guide. Formal legal provisions, exact quotations and emphasis may require full forms. Never set a contraction quota or add `honestly`, slang, jokes, emotional disclosures or a fabricated conversational persona.

When correspondence supplies shared context, answer the recipient's actual question before restating their background. Retain all distinct source facts within the rewrite scope. Without surrounding correspondence, do not presume that the reader knows an omitted fact.

## Re-express relationships, not just sentence order

For a paragraph that remains mechanical after reordering, draft from its propositions rather than rearranging intact sentences. Identify what happened, who did it, what remains unknown and what the reader needs next. Choose the clause structure that makes those relationships easiest to follow:

- **Carry known information into new information.** Let a clear referent connect adjacent sentences. Repeating the technical term can be better than an ambiguous `this`, `it` or a decorative synonym.
- **Combine related clauses when their relationship is established.** Integrate a condition with its consequence, or an observation with its precise limit. Do not add `because`, `therefore`, `despite` or `even` when they introduce a cause, inference, contrast or expectation absent from the source.
- **Recover concrete predicates.** Replace a cumbersome noun construction with the source's actor and verb, where known. Parse noun stacks before expanding them: `customer account deletion request` could concern a customer's request or deletion of an account. If context cannot resolve an attachment, preserve it and flag the ambiguity rather than inventing agency.
- **Control focus within the sentence.** Place the new result or action where it receives natural emphasis; keep negation, conditions and quantitative limits attached to the right claim. A dangling qualification at the end is not a substitute for a clear relationship.
- **Keep a useful pause.** A sequence of short complete sentences can read like a checklist; a long sentence can bury a contrast. Combine or separate by meaning, without length distributions, punctuation targets or compulsory fragments. Do not turn every pair into a semicolon or a colon plus caveat.

Synthetic examples (not benchmark fixtures or upstream quotations):

| Source | Possible rewrite | Fidelity boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `The lab has completed an inspection of the pump. The source of the leak remains unknown.` | `The lab has inspected the pump, but the source of the leak is still unknown.` | Inspection completed; source still unknown. Do not claim the inspection failed or assign blame. |
| `You may submit the form online. You must still bring the signed original to the appointment.` | `You can submit the form online, but you still need to bring the signed original to the appointment.` | Permission remains permission; the requirement and its timing remain. Retain exact legal wording where required. |
| `I enjoyed the workshop. I did not find the final exercise useful.` | `I enjoyed the workshop, though the final exercise was not useful to me.` | Preserve the mixed personal judgment; do not rewrite as unqualified approval or general failure. |
| `The trial did not measure energy use. It cannot establish whether the device saves energy.` | `Because the trial did not measure energy use, it cannot establish whether the device saves energy.` | This explicit evidential dependency is supported. Do not infer an energy saving from an unrelated result. |

These show grammatical options, not preferred templates. A source may express a fact more naturally than any replacement. Do not rewrite protected identifiers, numerical forms or terminology to increase lexical difference.

## Test the completed discourse

Read the paragraph openings together, then the endings. Do they move the reader through the actual subject, or repeatedly announce a topic and close with a generic lesson? Repair the specific redundancy. Keep needed navigation, a deliberate refrain and conclusions that convey real judgments or actions.

Check three contextual traps:

- A limitation followed by generic reassurance can erase the practical force of the limitation. Keep the real caution and any supported reassurance, without adding a cheerful reset.
- `From X to Y` needs a meaningful range, progression or contrast. Unrelated examples may be clearer as examples; preserve every substantive item rather than deleting one to avoid a pattern.
- A rebuttal may answer an alternative the source or reader never raised. Remove only empty staging; keep a genuine distinction, negative finding or rejected option.

Ask whether a polished sentence states a recoverable claim about this subject. Topic nouns alone do not make a generic sentence useful. Retain ordinary generalizations when they carry the writer's argument; request missing evidence only if needed for the task, and never replace a broad assertion with an invented number or anecdote.

Read the whole draft for idiomatic collocations, pronoun reference, tense and ownership. Restoring omitted facts should not leave a trail of awkward add-on caveats. If repair makes a sentence dense, recompose the surrounding paragraph from the original source again.

## Academic English: preserve the section's job

Apply only to the relevant section, without imposing a new paper template:

- Abstract/introduction: preserve the actual problem, gap, response and results; distinguish planned work from work already done.
- Related work: keep prior authors' findings distinct from the present contribution and retain attribution.
- Methods: preserve executable sequence, conditions and terminology. Procedural repetition can be necessary.
- Results: keep denominators, baselines, excluded cases and uncertainty next to the observation. Do not turn measurements into interpretations.
- Discussion/conclusion: identify interpretation as interpretation; retain limits and stated implications without inventing future work or a triumphant closing claim.

## Voice evidence and review feedback

If samples exist, infer each feature separately: a dictated sample may support cadence but not punctuation; a jointly edited report may not establish the writer's personal tone. Different channels can support different styles. With no sample, use appropriate English for this source's purpose rather than inventing distinctive traits.

For a requested audit, report an exact span, contextual reason and useful repair direction. Merge overlapping labels for the same defect; a valid exception does not become a defect under a synonymous label. Distinguish a plausible alternative from a necessary correction. Default delivery remains the finished prose, not this checklist.
