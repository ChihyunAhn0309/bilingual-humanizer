For eight weeks, I read a chapter in the morning before looking at my phone, expecting to remember more of what I read. By the end, I could recall the plots of three novels. But I hadn't tested my memory before the experiment, so I can't tell whether my recall improved.

The change I noticed was in how I read: I stopped interrupting a chapter to check messages. I liked that, although I still checked my phone as soon as I finished. I didn't keep to the routine every morning, either: on two mornings, I skipped reading altogether.

The reading itself was worthwhile to me even without evidence of a memory benefit. I'm reluctant to recommend the routine to everyone, though. My mornings were quiet during those weeks, and I didn't try it while travelling. Next month I want to try reading after dinner instead, although I haven't chosen a start date.
