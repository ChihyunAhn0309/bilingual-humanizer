# Fresh-context fidelity and editorial review

## Scope and method

I read the method, frozen manifest, and the source, baseline, and challenger files only. I did not consult an authoring log, a prior review, or detector output. Each candidate was compared directly with its source for actors, actions, objects, numbers, timing, conditions, modality, attribution, scope, speech acts, format, and voice. “Required repair” below is reserved for a meaning, scope, or speech-act problem; optional editorial differences are identified separately.

## 1. English incident notice

### Baseline

The chronology, thresholds, unaffected cases, mitigation, status-page update, cause, rollback, validation sample, restoration, unresolved test failure, Friday commitment, and customer instruction all survive. Every number and time matches. The only precision loss is “25 affected projects”: the source says **previously** affected projects, which makes clear that the checks happened after rollback and does not describe their state at check time. The chronology already makes the intended reading likely, so this is minor rather than a substantive contradiction.

The prose is efficient and appropriately operational, but it regularizes the source's voice. “To prevent,” “identified ... as the cause,” “failed to detect,” and the isolated final action paragraph sound more templated and institutional than the source's plainer “rather than risk,” “traced,” and “missed the fault.”

**Required repair:** none. For maximum precision, restore “previously” before “affected projects.”

### Challenger

All factual constraints are retained, including the exact times, 10,000-record boundary, PDF availability, Monday release, 25-project check, Friday owner and commitments, and the narrow re-export window. “Customers do not otherwise need to act” correctly preserves the exception structure of “No customer action is required, but ...”. “Equivalent coverage” is a fair paraphrase of “the same coverage.”

The challenger more clearly retains the source's connective and lexical habits: “rather than,” “traced,” “missed the fault,” “still don't know,” and the direct final instruction. “Projects ... received incomplete files” and checks that “returned” row counts are slightly less idiomatic than the source, but do not alter the incident.

**Required repair:** none. Optional polish would be “CSV exports for projects ... produced incomplete files” and “checks ... found complete row counts.”

### Preference

**Challenger, by a modest margin.** Fidelity is effectively tied, while the challenger better preserves the source's plain, accountable incident voice. The baseline is fully usable and arguably a little smoother in two places.

## 2. English withdrawal letter

### Baseline

The addressee, title, June programme, archive, demonstration material, May 12 deadline, practical-to-general consequence, non-complaint, two reviewers, autumn call, permissions review, request for confirmation, comment-retention preference, closing, and sender all remain. “Disqualify a new submission” is narrower and more procedural than “prevent a new submission”: a future submission might be prevented without formal disqualification. It therefore changes the scope of the requested assurance. “With the committee's agreement” also reads a little more like prior permission than the source's softer “if ... willing.”

The baseline preserves the formal letter format and is clear, but the imperative opening (“Please withdraw”) is more abrupt than the source's explanatory “I am writing to withdraw.” “Although” and “There is no need” retain the original logic well.

**Required repair:** replace “will not disqualify” with “will not prevent” (or equally broad wording). Optional voice repair: soften the opening and use “if the committee is willing.”

### Challenger

The material circumstances, dates, proposed-session distinction, non-complaint, appreciation, intended later submission, condition, confirmation request, and sign-off are all present. The first sentence drops the noun “workshop proposal,” though the title and context still identify the object. “Hope to submit” weakens the explicit request/intention in “would like to submit.” More importantly, “The current reviewer comments can remain” asserts acceptability or permission; the source says only that Mara does not need them removed. Those speech acts are not identical.

The challenger otherwise best preserves Mara's measured first-person voice: “I would like,” “I could,” “This is not a complaint,” and the direct “Please confirm.” “At this stage” is an unsupported shade of emphasis but is naturally entailed by “doing so now.”

**Required repair:** change the last sentence to “I do not need the current reviewer comments removed from your system” or a true equivalent. To preserve modality, prefer “would like to submit” over “hope to submit.” Adding “my workshop proposal” in the opening would remove the small object-label ambiguity.

### Preference

**Baseline, narrowly, as frozen.** Its “disqualify” requires broadening, but it preserves the final sentence's speaker-owned preference; the challenger's “can remain” changes that speech act. After that one repair, the challenger would be preferable on voice.

## 3. Korean neighborhood notice

### Baseline

The event date, 09:00–11:00 time, location, rain postponement, next-Saturday fallback, RSVP deadline and channel, tool allocation, prior crowding, three groups of four, child-count exception, edge-weeding assignment, and permission to leave early all survive. Two modality/speech-act changes matter. “정리합니다” turns “정리하려고 합니다” (an announced plan/intention) into a more categorical event statement. More clearly, “잡초 뽑기만 맡습니다” turns the source's polite instruction/request “맡아 주세요” into an assertion about what families do or are assigned.

The baseline also removes “두 시간이 길게 느껴질 수 있지만,” an empathetic acknowledgment that carries the source's accommodating voice and sets up the permission not to stay. “개인이 준비하고” and “가능하신 만큼 함께해 주세요” are accurate but more administrative/generic than “각자 가져오셔야 하고” and “가능한 만큼만 손을 보태.”

**Required repair:** restore the directive force of “맡아 주세요.” Restoring the planning modality in the opening is advisable. The omitted acknowledgment should also be restored if voice and pragmatic meaning are within the fidelity requirement, as the method says they are.

### Challenger

The challenger retains all dates, times, conditions, quantities, responsibilities, exceptions, and participation limits. Reordering the rain sentence does not change its scope. “오는 순서대로” is slightly less exact than “도착한 순서대로,” but in context has the same operational meaning. “쓰레기봉투는 주민회에서 준비하겠습니다” preserves the actor but changes a statement about what the residents' association will provide into a first-person commitment; unless the notice's speaker is established as speaking for that association, the source does not license that stance. “어린이를 인원에 포함하지 않고” can grammatically sound as though each family performs the counting, whereas the source leaves the allocator responsible for the headcount rule.

Voice preservation is strong: the planned, invitational opening, recollection of the last event, concession about two hours, permission to leave, and “손을 보태” all remain. The polite register and paragraph shape also match.

**Required repair:** use “쓰레기봉투는 주민회에서 준비합니다” to avoid assuming the speaker's authority/identity. For precision, use “도착한 순서대로” and formulate the child exception so the allocation rule, not the family, is the grammatical counter.

### Preference

**Challenger, clearly.** It needs one attribution/stance repair, but it retains substantially more of the source's accommodating neighborhood voice and does not omit the two-hour concession. The baseline's directive-to-assertion shift and voice loss are more consequential.

## 4. Korean exhibition review

### Baseline

All twelve maps, the one-hour doubt, rooms, reverse-side notes, port-name details, prices and weather, map-number problem, two missed identifications, three volume levels, chair, erased route, lack of flashiness, and quiet pace remain. Several evaluations shift, however. “생각보다 좋았다” becomes “예상보다 인상적이었다” (good becomes impressive); “마음에 남았다” becomes “기억에 남았다” (personal liking/affective residue becomes memorability); and “그 길을 다닌 사람” becomes plural “사람들.” The last change is small, but the first two alter the review's judgments. “메모까지” also introduces an “even the notes” emphasis absent from “메모를 함께.”

The rewrite is polished but noticeably more impersonal and critical-register formal: it removes “조금,” replaces “그런데/그래도” with “그러나/반면,” suppresses the final “나는,” and substitutes abstract phrases such as “시선이 갔다.” This is the largest voice drift among the baseline texts.

**Required repair:** preserve the positive judgment “좋았다” and the closing affect “마음에 남았다.” Restoring first person and the source's lighter connectives is needed for the stated voice-preservation criterion.

### Challenger

The objective details and all numbers are retained. The opening question usefully carries the writer's initial doubt, and the final two sentences closely preserve the source's personal, quiet conclusion. There are nonetheless two additions: “생각이 달라졌다” reports a change of mind rather than the source's specific judgment that the display method was better than expected, and “물가와 날씨를 따라가다 보니” invents an act/process of following those notes. “사람이 먼저 보였다” also changes the source's explicit contrast (“not the line, but the person”) into priority (“the person appeared first”). In the chair passage, “때문이다” makes causation explicit where the source presents an adjacent consequence; that inference is reasonable but unnecessary.

The challenger preserves the first-person, tentative, conversational review voice much better than the baseline, including “조금,” “아쉬웠다,” “그래도,” “반가웠다,” and the exact affective close.

**Required repair:** replace “생각이 달라졌다” with the source's specific favorable assessment, and remove the invented “따라가다 보니” process while restoring the line/person contrast. Optional precision: avoid the explicit “때문이다” causal packaging.

### Preference

**Challenger, with repairs.** As frozen, both candidates have evaluative fidelity defects. The challenger is closer in voice and preserves the final verdict exactly, while its two unsupported formulations are straightforward to remove. The baseline's systematic formalization changes both the opening praise and the closing personal evaluation.

## Overall assessment

On the four paired texts, my preferences are challenger for the incident notice, baseline narrowly for the letter as frozen, challenger for the neighborhood notice, and challenger for the exhibition review. This supports the voice-anchor hypothesis directionally, not conclusively: challenger G more often retains characteristic connective choices, directness, first-person presence, and lexical texture. It does **not** eliminate fidelity risk. In particular, preserving voice can coexist with newly asserted agency (“준비하겠습니다”), altered speech acts (“can remain”), and small invented cognitive processes (“따라가다 보니”). Those are ledger-check failures even when the result sounds more like the source.

## Limitations

- This is one fresh editorial judgment over four short, AI-produced pairs, not a blinded multi-rater study.
- I knew which directory was baseline and which was challenger, so method-label bias cannot be excluded.
- The genres, languages, and individual texts are confounded: there is only one item for each language–genre combination.
- Korean honorifics, subject omission, and institutional first-person forms permit contextual readings that the text alone cannot settle; I therefore flag ambiguity rather than claim an unequivocal factual contradiction in those cases.
- I assessed textual fidelity and editorial voice only. I did not assess detector performance, provenance, external factual truth, or whether the procedure was followed during drafting.
- A preference does not mean the selected text is publication-ready. The letter, neighborhood notice, and Korean review challenger all need the repairs identified above; the baseline letter also needs its broadened assurance restored.
