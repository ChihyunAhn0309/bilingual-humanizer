# Challenger G: voice-anchor editing

## Hypothesis

A rewrite may become generic even when every proposition survives. Before drafting, G therefore records a small set of **voice anchors** already present in the source: the writer's distance from the reader, recurring connective habits, preferred degree of directness, and one or two characteristic lexical choices. It then edits from a proposition-and-constraint ledger while deliberately retaining those anchors. This is a voice-preservation hypothesis, not a detector heuristic.

G does not rename the prior source-sensitive prototype: it neither preserves sentences merely because they appear effective nor routes document types into headings. It also differs from the earlier clause examples and given–new/topic-continuity prototypes. The intervention is the explicit, auditable voice-anchor ledger applied across four genres. Structure changes only where reader work requires it; there is no change quota, sentence-statistic target, word blacklist, invented imperfection, or detector feedback.

## Procedure

1. Record every actor, action, object, number, time, condition, modality, attribution, speech act, formatting constraint, and scope limit.
2. Record three or four voice anchors evidenced by the source. Do not infer biography or add a trait that is not on the page.
3. Draft for the same audience and purpose. Retain the anchors across the whole document, but do not mechanically copy a sentence or phrase.
4. Check the candidate against the ledger and source. Reject it before detector submission if any item changes.
5. Read once for voice drift. Make only defect-based repairs, record them, and freeze bytes.

The comparator uses the retained v1.5.1 skill as published, with the same source, writer context, one drafting pass, one self-review opportunity, and no detector feedback. Both arms are AI-produced.
