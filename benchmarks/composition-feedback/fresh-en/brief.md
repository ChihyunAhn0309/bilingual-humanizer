# Fresh English composition brief

Prepared before candidate drafting. This is one new-source development check of the experimental bilingual-humanizer method, not a statistical holdout. The source is known AI-authored synthetic prose, not the user's experience. Its first-person narrative facts remain binding.

## Reader and purpose

A general reader is following a restrained first-person reflection on preparing a useful short walking guide for a visiting friend. The deliverable remains a reflection, not the actual route guide. Keep the plain, candid voice, the supplied personal preference, and the distinction between experience, uncertainty and unfinished work. Do not invent a persona, emotion, memory, directions or completed checks.

## Declared reader route

Lead with the continuing wish to send the guide by the friend's Sunday visit. Bring the concrete pre-send accessibility requirement and its supporting river-road observation together near the opening. Then give the Wednesday walk and its limited timing evidence. Finish with the market observation, the required treatment of unconfirmed access hours, and the source's comparison between a useful checked/unchecked account and pretending a single walk answered everything.

Reader benefit: the unfinished action is visible before the retrospective observations, while those observations keep their local limits. Integrating the final comparison with the treatment of market information should reduce repeated commentary about how to write responsibly. This route is an editorial comparison, not a prediction of detector preference. There is no score target.

## Protected propositions and dependencies

1. The writer intended to make a short walking guide for a friend visiting Sunday and still wants to send something useful by Sunday.
2. The writer personally walked the route on Wednesday, station to old footbridge. Twenty minutes was the estimate; thirty-five minutes was the observed duration, partly due to stopping to check two street names.
3. No second walk was timed. Thirty-five minutes records that particular trip and is not a reliable estimate for everyone. Keep this restriction beside the duration.
4. The map was useful until the market. It marked a path behind the stalls, whose entrance was blocked by a locked gate when the writer arrived.
5. Whether the gate is normally locked or the visit occurred at the wrong time remains unknown. The writer should describe the observed situation in the guide and leave usual access hours unconfirmed. This communication requirement is separate from the unknown fact.
6. The writer liked the quieter road by the river. Its uneven pavement would make the writer hesitate to recommend it to someone using a wheelchair; no definitive inaccessibility finding is supplied.
7. An accessible alternative has not been checked. The writer needs to check whether one exists BEFORE SENDING the guide, rather than present a preferred route as suitable for everyone. Do not weaken the obligation or silently complete the check.
8. A clear note distinguishing checked from unchecked information would help more than pretending one walk settled every question. Preserve both sides and the comparative usefulness, even if the closing is moved or integrated.
