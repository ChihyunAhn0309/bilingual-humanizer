Decision memo: Digitization queue for the next quarter

For next quarter, the collections team at the Alderwick Museum proposes reserving 60 percent of digitization capacity for objects already requested by researchers. Fragile items awaiting condition review and a small selection for the education team would share the remaining capacity. This allocation would apply for one quarter before being reconsidered.

Our current queue contains 184 objects. The records, however, do not consistently show which requests remain active and which have already been answered through other means. Of the 25 entries reviewed last week, 7 appeared to need clarification. The sample came from the oldest records and may therefore overstate the problem across the full queue. We should confirm those requests before assigning photography dates.

Researchers need reliable delivery estimates, and conservators need enough time to assess objects that require preparation before they can be moved safely. Educators have also asked for images of ordinary household objects. These receive fewer individual requests but support several planned lessons. Ordering the queue by request date alone would not account for all three needs.

I recommend that the registrar use existing staff hours to prepare a provisional schedule by 18 November. Photography should start with confirmed requests that have no outstanding handling concerns. We have yet to agree on who may authorize an exception when an exhibition deadline conflicts with a conservation assessment. Until that responsibility is assigned, disputed objects should stay on the provisional schedule without a promised completion date.
