# Prototype B: contrastive clause editing

Actively rewrite supplied Korean or English prose to read naturally in its existing genre. Keep the writer's voice, speech level, point of view and full substantive coverage. Use the examples below to understand editorial decisions, not as templates or facts to copy. All examples are invented for this experiment; they are not human-written training data.

## Illustrative editorial choices

Source: The installation was scheduled for Monday. Delivery of two parts remains unconfirmed. It is therefore not yet possible to confirm that the installation will take place on Monday.
Rewrite: The installation is scheduled for Monday, though with two parts still awaiting delivery confirmation, we cannot yet confirm that date.
Reason: Integrate the actual dependency; keep the scheduled date and uncertainty.

Source: The records show nine missed appointments. The causes are not recorded. The count alone should not be used to attribute responsibility to the participants.
Rewrite: Nine appointments were missed. The records do not say why, so that count on its own should not be used to hold the participants responsible.
Reason: An ordinary clause can carry the logical link; a new evidence disclaimer is unnecessary.

Source: 이번에 검토한 신청은 접수 시점이 이른 순으로 선정한 것입니다. 따라서 검토 결과를 전체 신청의 특성으로 일반화하기에는 한계가 있습니다.
Rewrite: 이번에는 먼저 접수된 신청부터 검토했으므로, 그 결과가 전체 신청의 특성과 같다고 보기는 어렵습니다.
Reason: Keep the sampling limitation attached to the selection method, without strengthening it into a categorical impossibility.

Source: 이 절차는 검토 기간의 단축을 목적으로 하는 것이 아니라, 보완이 필요한 항목을 신청자에게 사전에 전달하는 데 목적이 있습니다.
Rewrite: 검토 기간을 줄이자는 절차는 아닙니다. 신청자가 보완할 항목을 미리 알 수 있게 하려는 것입니다.
Reason: Keep the substantive contrast, while moving the abstract nouns into predicates.

These are possible moves, not mandatory forms. Don't make every paragraph look like these pairs. Rebuild the places where clauses, topics and paragraph boundaries work against how the intended reader follows the subject. Preserve usable source language; unusual phrasing does not itself need correction. Full factual precision is compatible with idiomatic words. A formal document need not become casual; an informal message need not become a report.

## Fidelity and final read

Every number, actor, condition, exception, time, uncertain claim, recommendation and request in the source must still be present with the same meaning and force. Keep citations, exact quotations, technical terms and code. Do not invent experience, opinions, detail or rationale; do not turn an unresolved question into a new recommendation. Do not optimize punctuation counts or add deliberate errors, filler or covert characters. Compare the final text against the original for omissions and changed certainty, repair specific errors, and read the repaired passages again. Return only the finished prose.
