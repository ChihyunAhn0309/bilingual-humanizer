Decision memo: Next quarter's digitization queue

The collections team at the Alderwick Museum proposes setting aside 60 percent of next quarter's digitization capacity for objects researchers have already requested. The rest would go to fragile items awaiting condition review and a small selection for the education team. We would use this allocation for one quarter, then reconsider it.

Our queue has 184 objects, though the records do not consistently tell us which requests are still active and which have been answered in other ways. Last week, 7 of the 25 entries reviewed appeared to need clarification. Since the sample came from the oldest records, it may overstate how widespread the problem is in the queue. We should confirm those requests before setting photography dates.

Scheduling by request date alone would not account for all three groups' needs. Researchers need reliable delivery estimates, and conservators need enough time to assess objects that require preparation before they can be moved safely. Educators have requested images of ordinary household objects, which receive fewer individual requests but support several planned lessons.

I recommend that the registrar draw up a provisional schedule by 18 November within existing staff hours. Photography should start with confirmed requests that have no outstanding handling concerns. We still need to agree who may authorize an exception when an exhibition deadline conflicts with a conservation assessment. Until that responsibility is assigned, disputed objects should stay on the provisional schedule without a promised completion date.
