# Blinded final-genre review

Scope: only source.txt, X.txt, and Y.txt in fresh-blind/en-support and fresh-blind/ko-essay were read. Candidate labels remain blinded. This review separates fidelity from naturalness; it does not infer authorship.

## en-support

**Fidelity: X = Y. No material issue in either candidate.**

Both preserve the recipient and sender, product and version, Tuesday workshop and twelve participants, installation/restart times (9:14/9:16), shutdown times (10:42/11:07), responsive touch controls, one reproduction, and the second unit's four-hour run without failure. Both retain the distinction between an update worth investigating and an unestablished software/connector cause.

The practical conditions and speech acts remain intact: leave the panel off; no need to open it or recreate the workshop; replacement can be arranged without a delivery charge if the fault recurs after engineering supplies and verifies an update; replacement will instead be offered if an update is unavailable by Friday; a progress report is promised by Thursday 4 p.m., in the recipient's local time, even without a diagnosis. Neither candidate turns the conditional replacement into an unconditional shipment promise or claims that the recording was inspected. The diagnostic file's lack of storage errors stays separate from the unverified recording. The apology, inferred frustration, company test experience, and personal follow-up commitment remain correctly attributed.

**Naturalness: X = Y (tie).** Both read as credible, clear customer-support email. X uses contractions more consistently and retains the source's conversational sequence. Y puts the immediate instruction near the top and closes with the follow-up commitment, making the next steps particularly easy to locate. That is a useful genre-specific organization change, not a factual improvement. I would choose Y for support usability, with no meaningful confidence that its sentence-level prose is more natural.

Minimal repairs: none required.

## ko-essay

**Fidelity: X = Y. No material issue established.**

Both preserve the first-person arc and concrete details: the initial expectation about finishing books; last November's third Saturday; the small bookshop beside the laundry; arrival ten minutes before 2 p.m.; four people at the window; the concealed folded corner; Seonju's one-scene prompt; the early train-station scene; finishing December's book the previous day; Minho's judgment and the narrator's personal reaction; two additional coffees; missing the first week of January with a cold-related message and a stronger underlying burden; continuing disagreement while later recognizing some merit; returning on February 17; one vacant seat; hot water before an explanation; admitting the unfinished book before speaking; and Minho's own hesitation. Neither invents sensory detail, dialogue, reconciliation, an actual diagnosis of a cold, or a fully resolved attitude toward the group.

The final ambivalence also survives: Saturday-morning reluctance and longing for solitary quiet remain alongside a modestly increased interest in other readers. Both preserve who said what and whose feelings or interpretations are being reported. X's “끝까지 읽는 일” and Y's “끝내는 일” are contextually faithful naturalizations of “책 한 권을 완성하는 일.”

One **minor, ambiguous fidelity caution** in X: “막상 이야기가 시작되자” replaces “막상 이야기를 시작하니.” The source can suggest the narrator beginning to speak, while X explicitly describes the conversation starting. Korean's omitted subject also permits a collective reading of the source, so I do not count this as a demonstrated material error. If strict preservation of narrative agency is desired, the minimal repair is to restore “막상 이야기를 시작하니.”

**Naturalness: Y > X, narrowly.** Both sound natural and suit a restrained personal essay. Y better retains the source's reflective paragraph rhythm. X breaks “감기 기운이 있다는 메시지를 보냈지만, 사실은…” into separate statements and likewise separates the empty seat from Seonju's gesture; those changes produce a somewhat more clipped rhythm. This is an aesthetic preference, not evidence of a defect. Neither candidate adds an over-explicit lesson or smooths away the narrator's unresolved feelings.

Minimal repairs: none required; the optional X repair above would remove the small agency ambiguity.

## Limits

This is an AI textual comparison of two short cases, not a human-reader study or a general benchmark. Naturalness rankings are subjective and the Korean preference is slight. A close comparison can still miss semantic nuances, especially omitted subjects and contextual implications. The review checks candidate claims against the supplied sources only; it does not verify those sources against external events. It supports no conclusion about author identity, generation method, detector performance, or how either candidate would perform across other genres.
