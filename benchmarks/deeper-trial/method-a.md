# Prototype A: discourse and working prose

Rewrite the supplied text as a finished piece for its actual reader. Keep its language, genre, level of formality and writer's point of view. This is an active rewrite, not a summary.

Read the source for what the writer is doing: proposing, reporting, questioning, asking, describing, or entertaining. Let those acts determine the movement of the piece. In a memo, the writer is trying to settle specific work, rather than explaining the concept of good decision-making to an outsider. In an essay, follow the actual argument rather than importing a memo structure. Let the source's unresolved issue remain unresolved.

Compose direct, usable prose with the ordinary phrasing a competent colleague would put in this particular document. Give a closely related observation and qualification room in the same sentence, instead of making every fact a declaration followed by a separate caution. Spend words where the reasoning needs them. Where the reader can follow a connection, don't narrate that connection again. Retain an explicit relationship if removing it would change meaning. Paragraphs need not each begin with a summary and end with a lesson.

For Korean, build clauses around Korean predicates rather than abstract noun chains. Use topic continuity and natural ellipsis only when the actor remains clear. Keep the original speech level; formal Korean can still have flexible clause structure. For English, use ordinary verb phrases and the writer's established pronouns. Contractions are allowed where the source's genre permits them. Don't force punchy fragments or telegraphic lists.

Keep ALL source facts, distinct reasoning, quantitative relationships, conditions, uncertainty, attribution and speech acts. In particular a proposal stays a proposal, reported explanations stay reported, a recommendation isn't a completed action, and an inference stays qualified. Numbers, names and deadlines must retain their referents. Do not add anecdotes, feelings, imagined quotes, an invented narrator, evidence, examples or conclusions. Do not introduce mistakes, random slang, invisible characters or arbitrary sentence-length targets.

Check the completed text against the original in a separate pass. Correct missing or changed meaning locally, then read again for prose that has become stiff in the correction. Return only the finished text.
