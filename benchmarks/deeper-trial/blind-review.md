# Blinded review of three prose cases

This is an independent AI review, not a human preference study. I read only `source.txt`, `X.txt`, `Y.txt`, and `Z.txt` under the three specified blind-case directories. Rankings below describe these texts, not their authorship, production process, or detector performance. Fidelity and naturalness are assessed separately. Small stylistic preferences should not be treated as robust wins.

## Overall findings

No candidate introduces a material factual error, changes a numerical referent, or turns an unapproved proposal into an implemented policy. All retain the important uncertainty and evidence limitations. The clearest completeness issue is **ko-library Z**, which omits the source's explicit statement of the review's purpose and its place before the operating proposal is developed further. The substantive conditions themselves survive.

| Case | Fidelity | Naturalness/clarity | Overall ordering |
|---|---|---|---|
| en-museum | X = Y = Z on substantive meaning | X and Y are near-tied; Z has a small forward-reference weakness | X ≈ Y > Z, with only a slight gap |

| ko-library | Y ≈ X > Z for completeness | X and Y are near-tied; Z is readable but its reordered opening loses the stated review scope | Y > X > Z; Y/X are near-tied |

## en-museum

### Fidelity

All three preserve the collections team's proposal; the 60 percent share of next quarter's digitization capacity; the uses of the remaining capacity; and the one-quarter review horizon. They retain **184 objects** in the queue, **25 entries** in last week's sample, and **7 entries that appeared to require clarification**. They do not generalize that sample to the whole queue: its selection from the oldest records and its possible upward bias both remain explicit.

All preserve the three groups' needs, the author's recommendation to the registrar, **18 November**, existing staff hours, the conditions for beginning photography, the unresolved exception-authorizing role, and keeping disputed objects on a provisional schedule without a promised completion date. The memo register and recommendation speech act remain intact. I found no material repair needed in X, Y, or Z.

### Naturalness and clarity

- **X:** Clear, controlled memo prose. The shorter queue sentences and “Ordering the queue by request date alone” read naturally. It closely preserves the source's progression without sounding mechanically copied.
- **Y:** Equally effective. Moving stakeholder needs before the queue-data discussion makes the allocation rationale easy to follow. The final separate paragraph gives the unresolved decision appropriate visibility. These are legitimate structural changes, not omissions.
- **Z:** Natural and faithful overall. Its slight weakness is **“Scheduling by request date alone would not account for all three groups' needs.”** At that point the paragraph has not yet identified the three groups, and conservators have not previously been named as a group. Readers can resolve the reference from the next two sentences, so this is a local clarity issue, not a factual error. **Minimal repair:** move this sentence to the end of the paragraph, after the three groups have been introduced.

**Ranking:** X and Y are effectively tied; if forced to select, X has marginally smoother continuity from evidence to needs to action. Z is a very close third. No substantive fidelity distinction supports a large gap.

## ko-equipment

### Fidelity

All preserve a proposal for a **six-week** trial beginning **next month**, rather than announcing an approved launch. The aim remains access for teams with actual need and reuse of released slots, not full booking occupancy. **Eight registered devices** and **17 same-day cancellations last month** retain their correct referents. The inability to establish causes or unused time, the teams' attributed explanations, incomplete maintenance documentation, and the judgment against restricting particular teams on that basis all remain.

All retain the **four-hour** continuous booking rule for one team and one device, the requested information for longer measurements, the distinct development/analysis-team needs, the proposed **two-hour-before-start** cancellation rule, the treatment of unforeseen reasons, no cancellation-count penalties during the trial, and weekly review. All preserve the unresolved person and criteria for urgent priorities as a prerequisite to launch, followed by a request for team comments by **this Friday**.

Y's **“최대 4시간”** makes the source's already implied booking limit explicit; it does not change four hours into a new requirement. Y also retains the next sentence about longer measurements. This is not a material fidelity defect. I found no material repair needed in any candidate.

### Naturalness and clarity

- **X:** Its placement of the two team needs before the four-hour rule gives the policy paragraph a useful rationale-to-proposal progression. It preserves the formal proposal register and the closing request.
- **Y:** Readable and appropriately formal. “누가 어떤 기준으로 결정할지는” is clear. Some explanatory wording is longer than necessary, but not awkward enough to warrant correction. Its modest paraphrasing does not weaken the evidence cautions.
- **Z:** Also clear and restrained. The exact span **“반면 시료 도착 시간이 일정하지 않은 분석팀에는”** introduces a more explicit contrast than the source. The needs do differ, so it does not create a substantive contradiction, but the teams' needs need not be presented as opposed. **Optional minimal repair:** delete “반면 ”. This is a rhetorical preference, not a material meaning failure.

**Ranking:** X ≈ Y ≈ Z. A forced X, Y, Z order reflects very small organizational and connective preferences; it should not be reported as a decisive quality separation.

## ko-library

### Fidelity

All retain **six weeks**, **Tuesday and Thursday evenings**, and **20:00**; an unfinalized, time-limited proposal distinct from permanent policy; **42 submitted statements**, divided **28/9/5** by support/current-schedule preference/no preferred option; the limit on population-wide inference; and the inability to infer reasons from response counts.

All retain unresolved volunteer availability as a condition to address before operation, the quiet reading room's **12-person simultaneous capacity**, the distinction between hours and capacity, and the absence of current causal evidence that extended hours increase visits. All distinguish expressed preference from evidence of actual visits and preserve the need to check wording against an unintended permanent-policy implication.

### Actionable completeness issue: Z

The source explicitly states: **“이번 검토에서는 접수된 의견을 살펴보고, 운영안을 구체화하기 전에 확인해야 할 조건을 정리한다.”** Z has no equivalent. Its opening goes directly from **“지역 도서관의 시범 운영안은 6주 동안 화요일과 목요일 저녁 개관 시간을 20:00까지 연장하는 방안이다.”** to **“자원봉사자가 해당 시간에 참여할 수 있는지는 아직 해결되지 않았다.”**

This omits the review's declared purpose and the temporal relationship between this review and further development of the operating proposal. It does **not** falsely approve the proposal or remove the actual prerequisites. It is a bounded scope/speech-act omission, material to a strict preserve-every-element requirement but not a factual reversal. **Minimal repair:** insert the source's purpose sentence after Z's opening sentence, or use “이번 검토는 접수된 의견을 살피고, 운영안을 구체화하기 전에 확인할 조건을 정리한다.”

### Minor wording differences and naturalness

- **X:** Generally the most fluent local phrasing, particularly **“문을 늦게 닫더라도 동시에 이용할 수 있는 인원은 늘지 않는다.”** This clarifies the capacity point without changing it. A small modality change appears in **“이번 저녁 시간 연장과 향후 상시 운영 시간은 구분해서 논의해야 한다.”**, compared with the source's **“구분할 필요가 있다.”** The practical recommendation is unchanged, but “해야 한다” is firmer. **Optional minimal repair for exact modality:** use “구분해서 논의할 필요가 있다.” This is not a material policy change.
- **Y:** The strongest balance of completeness, source-level modality, and a consistent review-document register. Sentence splitting around the 42 responses is effective. Some source-like abstract phrasing remains, but it is appropriate to the genre and does not by itself signal poor quality.
- **Z:** The condition-first structure is readable and preserves the factual substance. It shifts discussion of the temporary-policy boundary to the end and omits the explicit review-purpose sentence. Neither the rearrangement nor the shorter length is inherently inferior; the missing scope statement is the reason for the fidelity penalty. “이 한계를 염두에 두면” is an acceptable paraphrase in context, not evidence that the stated causal limitation has been dropped.

**Ranking:** Y > X > Z for this preservation-focused task, with Y/X near-tied. X could reasonably be preferred on sentence-level fluency alone. Repairing Z's purpose omission would substantially narrow the overall gap.

## Interpretation limits

These are close candidates. The findings support one specific completeness repair and a few optional local edits, not a broad claim that one anonymous candidate family is consistently superior. No authorship classification or detector probability is inferred.
