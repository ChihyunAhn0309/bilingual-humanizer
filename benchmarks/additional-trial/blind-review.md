# Blinded editorial review

Scope: the source, A, and B files for `en-cache` and `ko-library` only. The assessment treats the texts as synthetic formal review documents and makes no authorship judgment. “Material” means a change that could alter a reader's understanding of a finding, constraint, uncertainty, or required action.

## en-cache

### Fidelity

**Material problems: none identified in either candidate.** Both retain all measurements and units, the 48-run scope, the missing distribution and mobile testing, the operational-context caveat, correlation rather than causal proof, the lack of rollout approval, and the rollback trigger of a failure rate **above** 2%. Neither invents an acceptable memory threshold or turns the rollback rule into deployment authorization. The instructions to consider both measurements and separate approval from evidence remain intact.

Minor nuances:

- **A strengthens one evidentiary formulation.** Source: “The review should avoid filling that gap with assumptions about consistency or exceptional cases.” A: “They therefore provide no basis for assumptions about consistency or exceptional cases.” This changes a review instruction into a categorical description of the figures' evidentiary value. Because both texts explicitly say the full distribution is absent, it does not materially change the warranted conclusion, but the source's more limited formulation is slightly better calibrated.
- **A makes the production conclusion more declarative.** Source: “It would be premature to treat the trial as confirmation of a production benefit.” A: “The trial does not confirm a production benefit.” The substantive limit is preserved; the source foregrounds provisionality whereas A states the present evidentiary conclusion. B's “It is too early to treat the trial as confirmation” stays closer to the original modality.
- **A compresses the explicit framing of the memo.** Source: “This memo reviews a fictional software cache trial and the questions that remain before any operational rollout.” A opens instead with “Operational rollout of the fictional software cache change has not been approved.” This is a change of emphasis, not an omitted operational safeguard. B also drops the explicit statement of the memo's purpose but begins directly with the trial measurements.
- No other consequential additions, omissions, causal shifts, or changes to reader instructions were found. B's “fell” and “rose” accurately express the directions already established by the numbers.

| Dimension | Preference | Strength | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Naturalness | A | Slight | Both suit a formal memo. A generally uses direct, economical sentences; B's “measurements provide a basis for discussing the trial” is comparatively generic. |
| Cohesion | A | Slight | A's opening establishes the pending decision, then moves through the tradeoff, limits, and rollback rule. B discusses the median and distribution before returning to the broader measurement presentation in paragraph two, which creates a small backward step. |

| Overall reader quality | A | Slight | A is a somewhat more purposeful decision memo. Its two modestly firmer evidentiary formulations are the main fidelity tradeoff; B is marginally closer to the source's modality. |

**Overall pair judgment: A, slight preference.** This is an editorial preference between two faithful versions, not a finding that B has a substantive defect.

## ko-library

### Fidelity

**Material problems: none identified in either candidate.** Both retain the six-week Tuesday/Thursday extension to 20:00, all 42 responses and the 28/9/5 split, the limits on representativeness and on inferring reasons, unresolved volunteer availability as a prerequisite, the 12-person reading-room capacity, and the need to explain hours and simultaneous capacity separately. Both distinguish stated preference from attendance evidence, preserve the absence of causal evidence for increased visits, and require temporary and permanent scheduling decisions to remain distinct in discussion and communication.

Minor nuances:

- **A recasts the document's stated purpose as a recommendation.** Source: “이번 검토에서는 접수된 의견을 살펴보고, 운영안을 구체화하기 전에 확인해야 할 조건을 정리한다.” A: “운영안을 구체화하기 전에 접수된 의견과 아직 확인이 필요한 운영 조건을 함께 살펴볼 필요가 있다.” The original describes what this review does; A tells the reader what should be examined. The recommended work is already supported by the source, so this is a small rhetorical/modality shift, not a new substantive obligation.
- **B omits that explicit purpose statement.** It moves directly from the schedule to “자원봉사자가 해당 시간에 참여할 수 있는지는 아직 해결되지 않았다.” The actual conditions and review instructions are still present, so the omission affects orientation rather than factual completeness.
- **B states the preference/evidence distinction more categorically.** Source: “의견서에 나타난 지지는 선호의 표현이며 실제 방문으로 이어졌다는 증거와는 구분해야 한다.” B: “의견서의 지지는 선호를 나타낼 뿐 실제 방문으로 이어졌다는 증거는 아니다.” This is a firmer formulation of the same distinction. It does not assert that visits failed to occur and does not create a new causal claim. A retains the closer instruction “실제 방문으로 이어졌다는 증거와는 구분해야 하며”.
- **A is slightly more categorical about representativeness.** Source: “도서관을 이용하는 모든 사람의 요구를 그대로 나타낸다고 볼 수는 없다.” A: “도서관 이용자 전체의 요구를 그대로 나타내는 수치는 아니다.” Both reject treating the submitted responses as a direct account of everyone's requirements; the retained “그대로” keeps the practical meaning aligned. B preserves the source's more explicitly inferential wording: “그대로 보여준다고 볼 수는 없다.”
- Moving the temporary/permanent distinction to the final paragraph does not remove it in either candidate. A additionally states the temporary status in its introduction; B's opening still identifies the proposal as a six-week pilot. Neither converts the pilot into a permanent or already approved operating policy.

## Result
