# Reader-led rewriting experiment

Rewrite the supplied document actively, in its original language and appropriate register. Deliver prose the intended reader can use. Do not explain how you rewrote it inside the document.

Before drafting, identify the practical question the document answers and what the reader should understand or do next. Let those questions determine the sequence. For a decision memo, distinguish the proposal, evidence, unresolved questions and decision conditions; for an essay, preserve its perspective and developing thought. Do not force the same arrangement on every genre.

Read the complete source and then write the document as a whole. Preserve sentences or phrases that already work. Recast the rest without mechanically following the original order. Use ordinary, precise predicates; connect related facts and their qualifications in the same passage. State each distinct instruction once where the reader needs it. A caution belongs beside its claim, rather than in repeated summaries. A memo can perform its review without repeatedly announcing that it is a review.

Preserve every substantive fact, limitation, opinion, attribution and requested action. Keep exact numbers, units, names, citations, quotations and technical identifiers. Preserve who does what, timing, conditions, certainty, negation and the strength of recommendations. Distinguish evidence from inference, preference from observed behavior, and a fallback condition from permission to act. Consolidate true repetition, but do not remove a distinct limitation or instruction for brevity. Do not add an experience, explanation, result, motive, decision or requirement absent from the source.

For English, prefer the nouns and verbs the reader would actually use in this setting. Avoid turning ordinary actions into abstract commentary. For Korean, choose idiomatic predicate and particle combinations and preserve speech level; avoid repeated abstract endings when a direct statement carries the same force. Preserve the source's personality where it exists. Do not manufacture personality, errors, colloquialisms, first-person ownership or unusual punctuation.

After completing the draft, compare it with the source for additions, omissions, changed causal claims and changed modality. Repair actual errors, then read the finished draft for flow and strained wording. A review need not trigger another rewrite. Return one finished text; record review counts separately if requested. Do not use detectors or claim human authorship.
