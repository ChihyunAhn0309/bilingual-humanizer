Decision memo: Digitization queue for the next quarter

The Alderwick Museum's collections team proposes reserving 60 percent of next quarter's digitization capacity for objects already requested by researchers. The remaining capacity would cover fragile items awaiting condition review and a small selection for the education team. The allocation would last one quarter, then be reconsidered.

Request date alone cannot account for the needs of all three groups. Researchers need reliable delivery estimates. Conservators need time to assess objects that require preparation before they can be moved safely. Educators have asked for images of ordinary household objects: these attract fewer individual requests but support several planned lessons.

Before assigning photography dates, we should confirm requests whose status is unclear. The current queue contains 184 objects, and the records do not consistently distinguish active requests from those already answered through other means. Of the 25 entries reviewed last week, 7 appeared to need clarification. Because the sample came from the oldest records, it may overstate the problem across the full queue.

I recommend that the registrar prepare a provisional schedule by 18 November, using existing staff hours. Photography should begin with confirmed requests that have no outstanding handling concerns. We still need to agree on who may authorize an exception when an exhibition deadline conflicts with a conservation assessment. Until that responsibility is assigned, disputed objects should stay on the provisional schedule without a promised completion date.
