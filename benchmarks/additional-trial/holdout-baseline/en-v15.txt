Decision memo: Digitization queue for the next quarter

The collections team at the Alderwick Museum proposes setting aside 60 percent of next quarter's digitization capacity for objects researchers have already requested. The rest would cover fragile items awaiting condition review and a small selection for the education team. The allocation would run for one quarter, then be reconsidered.

Ordering the queue solely by request date would not reflect the needs of researchers, conservators and educators. Researchers need reliable delivery estimates, while conservators need enough time to assess objects that cannot be moved safely without preparation. Educators have asked for images of ordinary household objects, which receive fewer individual requests but support several planned lessons.

Our queue currently holds 184 objects, but the records do not consistently distinguish active requests from those answered by other means. Last week, a review of 25 entries found 7 that appeared to need clarification. The sample came from the oldest records, so it may overstate the problem across the full queue. We should confirm those requests before assigning photography dates.

I recommend that the registrar prepare a provisional schedule by 18 November, using existing staff hours. Photography should start with confirmed requests for objects that have no outstanding handling concerns.

We still need to agree on who may authorize an exception when an exhibition deadline conflicts with a conservation assessment. Until that responsibility is assigned, disputed objects should stay on the provisional schedule with no promised completion date.
