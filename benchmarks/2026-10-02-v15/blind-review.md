# Blind comparative review

Scope: Only the six authorized source/A/B files for each of the English and Korean library/cache cases were read. Labels are evaluated within each case only. No skills, candidate identities, mappings, scores, external reports, or web material were consulted. This review concerns textual fidelity and reader quality; it makes no detector or authorship claims.

## English library

### Additions, omissions, modality, and voice

- Both candidates preserve every concrete parameter: 6 weeks; Tuesday/Thursday; 20:00; 42 forms divided 28/9/5; unresolved volunteer availability; quiet-room capacity 12; temporary status; no causal evidence for increased attendance.
- A omits the explicit statement of the memo's purpose: reviewing feedback and identifying what must be resolved before the proposal is workable. The substantive workability concern survives in its volunteer paragraph, so this is a small loss of framing, not of an operational fact. B retains the purpose statement.
- Neither adds a reason for a respondent's preference or claims that the forms represent all users. Both preserve the distinction between expressed preference and an actual visit.
- A's “It would be temporary” is slightly more declarative than the source's instruction to keep the pilot clearly defined as temporary. Its following sentence preserves the obligation to distinguish permanent changes. B retains the source's explicit “should be clearly defined.” This is a minor difference in rhetorical emphasis, not a substantive policy change.
- Both retain the formal, cautious administrative voice. Neither introduces a promotional or personal voice. B has a slight fidelity advantage because it retains the document's purpose and the explicit instruction concerning temporary status.

### Reader quality

- **Naturalness: B, slightly.** “Volunteer availability remains unresolved and cannot simply be left until after the pilot begins” compresses A's three successive moves—“needs attention,” “practical constraint,” and a decision “requiring clarity”—without obscuring the constraint. Both are idiomatic, and the difference is modest.
- **Cohesion: B.** The opening states the review's purpose; the final paragraph then links the decision, expressed interest, and evidentiary limitation. In A, the decision sentence sits in the volunteer paragraph before the feedback is introduced, and the ending is only a caveat. A remains logically organized but has a less complete decision-making arc.
- **Nonrepetitiveness: B, slightly.** B uses two sentences to establish the unresolved coverage issue and its decision relevance; A uses three. Both still repeat the distinction between the pilot and permanent hours in their openings, so neither is wholly free of redundancy.
- **Overall: B, a small advantage.** This is a difference in framing and flow, not accuracy of the core facts.

## English cache

### Additions, omissions, modality, and voice

- Both preserve the fictional status, 48 runs, median latency 120ms to 90ms, memory 256MB to 288MB, missing distribution, no mobile clients, correlation/causation distinction, absent rollout approval, and rollback only for a failure rate **above** 2%.
- B omits the source's explicit statement that this memo reviews the trial and unresolved questions before rollout. The later paragraphs supply the substantive questions, so the omission affects framing rather than factual coverage. A retains it.
- B says “It is too early to claim a production benefit,” whereas the source says it would be premature to treat the trial as “confirmation of a production benefit.” The former can discourage even a qualified benefit claim; the latter specifically rejects confirmation. This is a small but real strengthening of the caution. A keeps “confirmation of a production benefit.”
- Neither invents a causal latency improvement, a performance distribution, an acceptable memory budget, or an approval decision. “Fell” and “rose” simply express the directions already given by the figures.
- Both preserve the restrained technical-review voice. A is marginally closer to the source's evidentiary wording and memo framing.

### Reader quality

- **Naturalness: tie.** B has a direct, readable opening (“Across 48 runs…”); A has a natural memo introduction. B's “It is too early to claim…” is conversationally smooth but less exact, so it should not earn an unqualified quality advantage.
- **Cohesion: A, slightly.** A combines “presented and weighed together” in one guiding sentence, then explains the memory tradeoff. B adds the generic sentence that measurements “provide a basis for discussing the trial” before separately stating presentation and decision instructions. A's progression is tighter.
- **Nonrepetitiveness: A, slightly.** B refers to “the next decision” once in paragraph two and again in paragraph four. A also repeats the obligation to weigh both measurements at the end, but its middle paragraph contains less meta-commentary (“provide a basis,” “Reviews should,” “the next decision should”). The difference is small; both retain some source repetition.
- **Overall: A, a small advantage.** Greater evidentiary precision and a tighter middle outweigh B's more immediate opening.

## Korean library

### Additions, omissions, modality, and voice

- Both preserve all schedule, duration, response-count, staffing, capacity, temporary-policy, and causal-evidence facts. Both retain the Korean source's explicit warning that response counts do not reveal respondents' reasons.
- A omits “이번 검토에서는 … 운영안을 구체화하기 전에 확인해야 할 조건을 정리한다.” B preserves that purpose and the timing of the review. A still makes clear that volunteer availability must be addressed before operation; no operational condition disappears.
- A moves the explicit temporary-versus-permanent explanation to the end. The first sentence still specifies 6 weeks, so the temporary nature is not contradicted; however, a reader scanning the opening does not immediately receive the explicit policy distinction. B keeps that distinction up front and moves the document-wording check there too.
- Both preserve the need to explain access hours separately from simultaneous quiet-room capacity, consider both preference groups, and examine wording that might imply a permanent decision. Neither introduces representativeness or attendance claims.
- There is no material change in obligation or certainty. Both retain the formal review voice. B is slightly more faithful to the memo's stated purpose; otherwise fidelity is effectively tied.

### Reader quality

- **Naturalness: tie.** A's “이 한계를 염두에 두면” is more natural than B's source-retained “이 한계를 유지하면.” B's feedback paragraph, especially “접수된 의견서 42건 중…,” is more economical than A's separate total and breakdown sentences. These advantages offset each other.
- **Cohesion: B, slightly.** B begins with purpose and temporary status, develops constraints and evidence, and ends with the decision's volunteer requirement. A's five topic blocks are easy to scan, but “이후 판단에서는…” arrives before the feedback, while the policy framing arrives only at the end.
- **Nonrepetitiveness: tie.** A has consecutive reminders of unresolved volunteer availability and checking availability. B spreads that reminder into its closing decision sentence but retains similarly repetitive policy qualifications in its opening. Neither has a decisive compression advantage without losing source emphasis.
- **Overall: B, narrowly.** The advantage is document-level organization, not uniformly better sentence-level Korean. A is competitive and has the more idiomatic wording for keeping an evidentiary limitation in mind.

## Korean cache

### Additions, omissions, modality, and voice

- Both preserve the fictional trial, 48 runs, all four measurement endpoints and units, median/distribution limits, operating-context requirement, untested mobile clients, correlation/causation limitation, pending approval, and a rollback threshold strictly exceeding 2%.
- A omits the opening description of the memo's purpose; B preserves it. A also removes the general sentence that looking at the two measurements together is the starting point for understanding the trial. The repeated concrete duty to present and evaluate both measurements remains, so no distinct technical requirement is lost.
- Both preserve the instruction not to present conditions absent from the summary as established facts. B phrases it as “덧붙이지 않도록 유의할 필요가 있다,” slightly softer in tone than A's “보태지 않아야 한다,” but neither authorizes adding unsupported conditions. The source itself uses “필요하다,” so this is a tonal difference rather than a clear change of rule.
- Both preserve the requirement to state limitations alongside positive figures, and both keep conclusions about mobile behavior open. Neither substitutes a production-benefit claim for the provisional trial result.
- Both remain formal and cautious. B retains more of the source's document framing; substantive fidelity is tied.

### Reader quality

- **Naturalness: B, slightly.** B's “48회 시험에서…” and “메모리 사용량 증가를 수용할 수 있는지는 운영 환경과 사용 조건까지 살펴야 판단할 수 있다” are compact and idiomatic. A's “가상의 … 시험을 48회 진행한 결과…” is also sound, but “어느 운영 환경에서 어떤 조건으로 사용할지를 살펴봐야 판단할 수 있다” is longer without adding information.
- **Cohesion: B, slightly.** B integrates the absent mobile clients with the need to withhold conclusions in one sentence, then develops the causal limitation. A states mobile behavior is unresolved and immediately repeats that the next decision must withhold a mobile conclusion, interrupting that progression.
- **Nonrepetitiveness: tie, with different weaknesses.** A repeats the mobile caution in consecutive sentences. B avoids that local repetition but retains the generic “두 수치를 나란히 보는 것이 … 출발점이다” directly after saying to present and review both figures, and repeats the mobile conclusion in the final paragraph. B redistributes repetition more gracefully; it does not decisively remove it.
- **Overall: B, a small advantage.** Better local compression and progression, with no substantive accuracy advantage and some retained redundancy.

## English holdout: repair-table essay

- **Material errors: none in either candidate.** Both retain the lamp/cable mismatch, Lena Ortiz, this month's intake change, all three intake questions, 90 minutes, 12 accepted items, three set aside for specialist tools, handwritten tally attribution, higher previous attendance, and the explicitly imperfect comparison. Both preserve the practical advice and first-person closing reflection. Neither claims the intake change caused a measured improvement.
- **Minor addition:** A makes intake timing explicit with “Before starting work.” That is a reasonable clarification of “intake conversation,” not a new outcome or material factual claim. No meaningful omissions in either candidate.
- **Naturalness: A, slightly.** Its opening keeps the joke in a flowing two-sentence account. B's “It felt efficient. Then…” is readable but more staccato, and “bring its loose parts with it” repeats the bringing action more heavily than A's “include its loose parts.”
- **Cohesion: tie.** A keeps the process and its initial observations together. B separates them into two paragraphs, which gives the figures and comparison caveat more room. Both organizations suit this short essay, and both preserve its movement from anecdote to process, advice, and reflection.
- **Nonrepetitiveness: tie.** No substantial repeated ideas were introduced or removed. A is slightly leaner in a few phrases; B's additional paragraph does not create substantive redundancy.
- **Overall: A, narrowly; substantive fidelity tied.** Both preserve the writer's warm, practical voice, including the distinctive final contrast. There is no material reason to reject either.

## Korean holdout: umbrella essay

- **Material errors: none in either candidate.** Both preserve the speaker's experience, named repair-shop owner 정미선, last-month conversation, four sound umbrellas, tray location and handle direction, cloth bag and broken-umbrella separation, two-week observation, limited rain and family-awareness caveats, drying procedure, and preference for practical access over appearance. Neither claims lasting success or invents an experience.
- Both simplify the original “비를 피하는 도구보다 도구를 찾는 일이…” into a comparison between avoiding rain and finding umbrellas. This makes the original comic contrast more explicit while retaining its point. It is a small phrasing change, not a material factual change.
- **Naturalness: B, slightly.** “이제는 다음 행동을 쉽게 만드는 일에 더 가깝다고 느낀다” is smoother and more syntactically complete than A's “지금은 다음 행동을 쉽게 만드는 일에 더 가깝게 느껴진다.” B also retains the unforced temporal phrase “나갈 때”; A's “나가다” is acceptable but adds no improvement.
- **Cohesion: B, slightly.** Separating the changed idea of tidiness into its own short paragraph gives the final concrete scene—soil marks, different umbrella colors, an umbrella within reach—a clear concluding role. A's single final paragraph is still coherent, and its “그렇다고” explicitly connects the thought and scene.
- **Nonrepetitiveness: tie.** Both retain essentially the same useful scene details and avoid new thematic repetition.
- **Overall: B, narrowly; substantive fidelity tied.** The preference rests on one clearer reflective sentence and paragraph pacing, not a difference in factual reliability or authenticity.

## Concise decision summary

No candidate in these six cases contains a material factual error. The main fidelity differences are omitted memo-purpose framing and the English cache B wording that broadens “confirmation of a production benefit” to “claim a production benefit.” That wording is a minor caution-strengthening, not a blocking error. Overall reader-quality preferences are English library B, English cache A, Korean library B, Korean cache B, English holdout A, and Korean holdout B; all margins are small and labels have been assessed separately within each case.
