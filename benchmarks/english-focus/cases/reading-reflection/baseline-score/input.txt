For eight weeks, I tried a morning routine: reading a chapter before looking at my phone. I expected it to help me remember more of what I read. At the end, I could recall the plots of three novels, but I had not tested my memory before the experiment, so I cannot tell whether my recall actually improved.

The change I noticed was simpler: I stopped interrupting a chapter to check messages. I liked that. I still checked my phone as soon as I finished. I did not follow the routine consistently: on two mornings I skipped reading altogether.

My mornings were quiet during those weeks, and I did not try the routine when I was travelling, so I am reluctant to recommend it to everyone. The reading itself was worthwhile to me even without evidence of a memory benefit. Next month I want to try reading after dinner instead, although I have not chosen a start date.
