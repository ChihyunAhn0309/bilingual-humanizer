For eight weeks, I read a chapter before looking at my phone in the morning. I expected the change to help me remember more of what I read. At the end of the eight weeks, I could recall the plots of three novels, but I had not tested my memory before the experiment. I therefore cannot tell whether my recall actually improved.

The change I noticed was simpler: I stopped interrupting a chapter to check messages. I liked that. I still checked my phone as soon as I finished, and on two mornings I skipped reading altogether. This was a routine I tried, not a rule I consistently followed.

I am reluctant to recommend the same routine to everyone. My mornings were quiet during those weeks, and I did not try it when I was travelling. The reading itself was worthwhile to me even without evidence of a memory benefit. Next month I want to try reading after dinner instead, although I have not chosen a start date.
