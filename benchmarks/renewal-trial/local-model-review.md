# Independent source-fidelity review: English museum memo

Scope: Compared only `sources/en-museum.txt` and `local-model/en-museum.txt`. This review assesses meaning, factual details, conditions, strength, completeness, register, and readability. No detector results or authorship assumptions were used.

## Conclusion

**Not fit for a source-faithful detector comparison without repairs.** The candidate preserves the broad subject, headline numbers, deadline, and scheduling proposal, but changes several decision-relevant propositions. In particular, it changes who handles exceptions, removes the restriction to disputed objects, and loses the direction of the sampling caveat. It also introduces conspicuous grammatical and referential problems.

## Specific findings

| Source meaning | Candidate wording or treatment | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Reserve 60 percent of next quarter's digitization **capacity**. | "60 percent of the next quarter's digitization facility" | The percentage and period survive, but a facility is not capacity. The operational quantity being allocated is mistranslated. |
| The allocation applies for one quarter **before being reconsidered**. | "then the possibility of it being reviewed again" | Weakens a planned reconsideration into a mere possibility and leaves the sentence grammatically incomplete. |
| Queue **records** do not consistently distinguish active requests from requests answered through other means. | "184 objects, but these do not always indicate if there are still pending or otherwise requests have been answered" | The intended issue remains partly recoverable, but the records are replaced by an unclear "these" and the distinction is no longer stated coherently. |
| A sample of 25 **entries** included 7 that appeared to need clarification. | A sample of 25 **objects** included 7 **objects** requiring clarification. | Numbers and tentative status are retained, but the unit changes from records/entries to objects. The source identifies uncertainty in request records, not the physical objects. |
| Because the sample came from the oldest records, it **may overstate** the problem across the queue. | It "may not be accurate for all records in the queue." | Loses the direction of the possible bias. A caveat about overestimation becomes an unspecific accuracy concern. |
| Confirm **those requests** before assigning photography dates. | "confirm this" before assigning dates. | Removes the explicit action target. "This" could refer to the sample or its accuracy rather than the requests. |
| Researchers need **reliable delivery estimates**. | "There are times when" researchers need information about "the time of arrival." | Adds an intermittent condition to an unqualified need and substitutes arrival information for delivery estimates. |
| Conservators need time to **assess** objects before they can safely be moved. | Conservators need time to **handle** such objects. | Changes assessment work into handling work, which matters to the scheduling rationale. |
| Ordinary household objects receive **fewer individual requests** but support **several planned lessons**. | "these are answered less often" but support "some planned curriculum teaching." | Changes low request volume into low response frequency. It also loses the specific quantity and form of the educational use: several lessons. |
| A date-only queue would not reflect **all three needs**. | It would not address "all of our needs." | Removes the explicit connection to the three stakeholder needs and broadens the claim. |
| Agreement is needed on who may **authorize an exception** when an **exhibition deadline** conflicts with a **conservation assessment**. | Agreement is needed on who may **apply for exceptions** when "a deadline, such as an exhibition" conflicts with "the needs of the conservator." | Material actor and policy change: the source concerns the approver, while the candidate concerns the applicant. The trigger broadens from an exhibition deadline to deadlines generally, and an assessment becomes a person's unspecified needs. |
| Until responsibility is assigned, **disputed objects** remain provisionally scheduled without a promised completion date. | "Until then, we require objects to remain" scheduled without a completion promise. | Omits "disputed," apparently extending the restriction to all objects. "Until then" also lacks the source's explicit dependency on assignment of responsibility. |

## Preserved information

- Museum and collections-team identity, the 60 percent allocation, the next-quarter period, and the broad division between researcher requests, fragile items, and education.
- The queue total of 184, the sample size of 25, the count of 7, review timing of last week, and selection from the oldest records, subject to the unit and caveat problems above.
- The registrar's provisional schedule, the 18 November deadline, and use of existing staff hours.
- The general instruction to start photography with confirmed cases without outstanding handling issues, although "those" is less precise than "confirmed requests."

## Completeness, language, and readability

All four main paragraphs have counterparts, so there is no wholesale paragraph omission. Nevertheless, several conditions and distinctions within them are lost or changed. "I recommend" becomes "I suggest," a modest weakening of the memo's recommendation; the title also drops the explicit "Decision memo" label.

The candidate remains English and broadly resembles an administrative memo, but it is less clear and less polished than the source. Examples include the comma between the subject and verb in "The collections team of Alderwick Museum, is suggesting," the fragment "and then the possibility of it being reviewed again," the malformed pending-request sentence, and the unclear referents "this," "these," and "those." Repeating "Until then" in the final two sentences obscures the dependency between assigning approval authority and holding disputed objects. These issues would themselves confound a comparison intended to isolate stylistic revision while holding source meaning and writing quality reasonably constant.

# Follow-up review: Korean library proposal

This is a follow-up by the same reviewer, not a new blind review. For this pass, only `sources/ko-library.txt` and `local-model/ko-library.txt` were read. Neither text was edited, and no detector was run.

## Conclusion

**The unmodified Korean candidate is not eligible for a source-faithful detector comparison.** It omits the two operating days, assigns the 12-person limit to an incoherent volunteer-related phrase instead of the quiet reading room, and reverses a central qualification about permanent schedule changes. Extensive grammatical corruption makes several other claims unresolvable. Repairs would have to reconstruct substantial portions of the source.

## Concrete fidelity findings

| Source | Candidate | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| A local library proposes extending evening opening to 20:00 on **Tuesdays and Thursdays for six weeks**. | Retains 20:00 and six weeks, but says “현재 지속된 연장 개관이 6주 동안 지속되도록 할 것을 정한다.” | Both weekdays disappear, as does the explicit evening framing in this sentence. The candidate introduces an implication that extended opening already exists and is to continue, rather than clearly describing the proposal. The local-library identification is also absent from the opening. |
| This review examines received comments and organizes conditions before the proposal is elaborated. | “이번 검토를 마친 후 … 조건 등을 정리해야 한다.” | Moves the condition-organizing work to after the review and omits the explicit purpose of examining the submitted comments. |
| The six-week trial is not a finalized permanent policy; discussion of the trial and future regular hours must be distinguished. | “영구적인 운영 안정적인 운영 정책” and discussion of “세부적인 조건” or “의견.” | The initial temporary-policy disclaimer remains partly recognizable, but the explicit distinction between trial extension and permanent hours becomes malformed and blurred. |
| 42 submissions: 28 support later opening, 9 prefer the present schedule, and 5 have no preferred option. | All four counts survive. Adds “추적을 하고 있으며”; uses “늦게 개관시간.” | The numerical distribution is retained, but tracking is an unsupported addition. The wording for later closing/longer opening is ambiguous and could suggest opening late. |
| Counts describe the distribution of submitted opinions, not necessarily the needs of all library users. | “이와는 반대로 … 분석을 통해 … 확인” and “도서관 사용자가 각각의 요구를 정확히 반영한 것은 아니다.” | Introduces an unsupported contrast and an analysis narrative. The representativeness caveat is only partially recoverable; its scope shifts from all users to an unclear statement about each user's needs. |
| Do not assume response counts reveal the reasons behind each opinion. | “접수된 의견서를 한 기준으로 의견의 이유를 정확하게 고려하지 못한다면, 각각의 의견을 고려하지 못할 것이라 해도 무방하다.” | Replaces the clear limit on inference from counts with a circular conditional. The source's caution is not preserved coherently. |
| Volunteer availability remains unresolved and cannot readily be deferred as a detail to settle after operations begin. | “아직까지는 여려가 처리되지 않았다” and a general instruction to check conditions. | Unresolved availability is partly inferable, but the explicit requirement to resolve this operational prerequisite before starting is lost. |
| **The quiet reading room has capacity for 12 people.** | “지금 자원봉사자의 수령의 정원은 12명이다.” | Critical entity error: the number is attached to a nonsensical volunteer-related phrase. The quiet reading room is omitted. The source does not state that volunteers number 12 or have a 12-person limit. |
| Longer opening does not increase the room's capacity; guidance must separately explain opening hours and the room's simultaneous occupancy. | “연장 시간을 지켜주면서 저녁의 인원이 늘어나는 것은 아니다” and “지속적인 양상을 확인할 수 있는 인원.” | Loses the distinction between time availability and simultaneous room capacity. The candidate's population and restriction cannot be identified reliably. “현재 약속된 이용 안내” also adds an unsupported prior promise. |
| No causal evidence in the current material shows that extending opening **causes more visits**. | No causal material for “연장 시간을 지속적으로 이용할 수 있는 인원을 증가시키는.” | Changes the outcome from visits to an unclear count of people able to use extended hours continuously. This does not preserve the source's causal claim precisely. |
| Expressed support is a preference, distinguishable from evidence of actual visits. | Refers to “실질적인 이용에 의한 증언을 통해 … 정리.” | The preference/evidence distinction is lost. The reference to testimony from actual use may imply an evidentiary basis the source does not provide. |
| Wording should prevent a trial decision from being read as also finalizing a permanent schedule change. | “영구적인 일정 변경까지는 확정되지 않을 수 없다.” | The ordinary reading of the double negative is that the permanent change cannot remain unfinalized, directly contradicting the source's central limitation. The final sentence does not repair this reversal. |

## Readability, register, and completeness

The candidate is predominantly Korean and retains formal endings, but it is not a usable administrative review. The four source paragraphs are collapsed into one block. Redundant phrases such as “연장 개관시간이 … 연장,” “운영 안정적인 운영,” and repeated “분명히 분명히 잘” impede reading. “여려가 처리되지 않았다,” “자원봉사자의 수령의 정원,” and “현재 시간에 선호하는 의견이란 과정” are not coherent standard prose. Referential and syntactic problems often prevent a reader from deciding which condition, person, space, or decision is meant.

Some anchors remain: 20:00, six weeks, the 42/28/9/5 distribution, the number 12, and a final mention of volunteer availability. Retaining these strings does not preserve their full propositions: the operating days are missing, 12 is bound to the wrong entity, and a permanent-policy qualification is reversed. The source's complete reasoning about scheduling, room capacity, volunteer prerequisites, representativeness, and causality therefore does not survive.
