Decision memo: Digitization queue for the next quarter

Proposed allocation

The collections team at the Alderwick Museum proposes reserving 60 percent of next quarter's digitization capacity for objects already requested by researchers. The rest would cover fragile items awaiting condition review and a small selection for the education team. The allocation would run for one quarter before being reconsidered.

Researchers need reliable delivery estimates, while conservators need enough time to assess objects that cannot safely be moved without preparation. Educators have asked for images of ordinary household objects, which receive fewer individual requests but support several planned lessons. Ordering the queue solely by request date would not reflect all three needs.

Requests to confirm before scheduling

The queue currently contains 184 objects. Its records do not consistently distinguish active requests from those already answered through other means. Of the 25 entries reviewed last week, 7 appeared to need clarification. Because the sample came from the oldest records, it may overstate the problem across the full queue. We should confirm those requests before assigning photography dates.

Provisional schedule and authority for exceptions

I recommend that the registrar prepare a provisional schedule by 18 November, using existing staff hours. Photography should begin with confirmed requests that have no outstanding handling concerns.

We still need agreement on who may authorize an exception when an exhibition deadline conflicts with a conservation assessment. Until that responsibility is assigned, disputed objects should remain on the provisional schedule without a promised completion date.
