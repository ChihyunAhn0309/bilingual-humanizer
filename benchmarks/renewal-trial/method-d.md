# Working decision documents

Use this route only for a memo whose reader must compare proposals, check conditions or act on an unresolved decision. Preserve all facts, figures, scope, uncertainty, explanations, requests and obligations, including the review's remit and stage. Do not add reasoning or experience that is absent from the source.

Rewrite the memo as a document the recipient can work from. Group each proposition by its practical role: proposed arrangement, evidence that needs interpreting, conditions to settle before acting, and instructions for communicating the decision. Choose the few headings or lists that help this particular recipient find those roles. Put a qualification with the relevant evidence and an unresolved responsibility with the affected action. Use ordinary complete sentences within that form; do not create telegraphic fragments or mechanical lists merely to change the appearance.

Active structural rewriting is appropriate here. The aim is to make the document usable without a second read. Merge true repetition, but preserve separate obligations and the force of each recommendation. Express the source's actions directly and keep its language and register. Do not turn a proposal into an approval or replace a substantive opening with a summary that loses its purpose.

After drafting, separately check every original point for coverage and strength, then review the finished memo for natural phrasing and useful navigation. Correct actual problems. There are no sentence-length quotas, statistical targets, or detector instructions. This route is not a template for fiction, essays or ordinary correspondence.
