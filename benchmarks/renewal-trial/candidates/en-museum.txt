Decision memo: Digitization queue for the next quarter

The collections team at the Alderwick Museum proposes reserving 60 percent of next quarter's digitization capacity for objects already requested by researchers. The rest would cover fragile items awaiting condition review and a small selection for the education team. This allocation would apply for one quarter before being reconsidered.

Researchers need reliable delivery estimates, while conservators need enough time to assess objects that require preparation before they can be moved safely. Educators have asked for images of ordinary household objects. These receive fewer individual requests but support several planned lessons. A queue based only on request date would not reflect all three needs.

The queue contains 184 objects, but the records do not consistently distinguish active requests from those already answered through other means. Of the 25 entries reviewed last week, 7 appeared to need clarification. The sample came from the oldest records, so it may overstate the problem across the full queue. We should confirm those requests before assigning photography dates.

I recommend that the registrar prepare a provisional schedule by 18 November, using existing staff hours. Photography should begin with confirmed requests that have no outstanding handling concerns.

We still need to agree on who may authorize an exception when an exhibition deadline conflicts with a conservation assessment. Until that responsibility is assigned, disputed objects should stay on the provisional schedule without a promised completion date.
