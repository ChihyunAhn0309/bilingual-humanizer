In this fictional software cache trial, median latency fell from 120ms to 90ms across 48 runs, while memory use rose from 256MB to 288MB. This memo reviews those results and the questions still open before any operational rollout. The two measurements need to be presented together: concentrating on latency alone would omit the increase in memory use and make the result harder to assess in context.

The lower median latency is relevant to further evaluation, but it does not describe every run. The supplied figures leave out the full distribution, so they cannot support assumptions about how consistent the results were or what happened in exceptional cases. Higher memory use represents a tradeoff; whether it is acceptable depends on the operating environment. The trial summary does not provide the context needed to make that judgment.

Mobile clients were not included, leaving their behavior unexamined. Any account of the findings should keep to the scope actually tested. The observed relationships are correlations and do not prove what would happen in production. They cannot yet confirm a production benefit, nor justify assuming that the same balance of latency and memory use would hold in conditions outside the trial.

Operational rollout has not been approved. A failure rate above 2% is the specified rollback condition and should remain explicit in further reviews of the proposed change. Having that rule does not authorize deployment or overcome the limits of the evidence. The next decision should consider the latency and memory changes together, retain the stated testing scope, and treat approval separately from the interpretation of these preliminary results.
