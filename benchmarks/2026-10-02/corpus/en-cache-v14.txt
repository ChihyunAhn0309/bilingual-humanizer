This memo reviews a fictional software cache trial and the questions that remain before an operational rollout. Across 48 runs, median latency fell from 120ms to 90ms, while memory use rose from 256MB to 288MB. The lower median is relevant to further evaluation, but does not describe every run. The supplied figures do not show the full distribution, so the review should not fill that gap with assumptions about consistency or exceptional cases.

The latency and memory measurements should be presented and weighed together. Focusing only on latency would leave out the memory increase and make the trial harder to assess in context. Judging whether this tradeoff is acceptable for a particular operating environment requires more operational context than the trial summary provides.

No mobile clients were included. The review should keep the findings within the scope actually tested and leave mobile behavior as an open question. The observed relationships are correlations, not causal proof of what would happen in production. It would be premature to treat the trial as confirmation of a production benefit or assume the same balance of latency and memory under untested conditions.

Operational rollout has not been approved. Any further review should explicitly retain the specified rollback condition: a failure rate above 2%. That rule does not authorize deployment or resolve the limits of the evidence. The next decision should keep approval separate from the interpretation of these preliminary results, while weighing both measurements and preserving the stated testing scope.
