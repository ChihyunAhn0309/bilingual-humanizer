This memo reviews a fictional software cache trial and the questions that remain before any operational rollout. Across 48 runs, median latency changed from 120ms to 90ms, while memory use changed from 256MB to 288MB. These measurements provide a basis for discussing the trial, but they should be presented together. A review focused only on latency would leave out the accompanying increase in memory use and would make the result harder to assess in context.

The lower median latency is a relevant observation for further evaluation. However, a median does not describe every run, and the supplied figures do not show the full distribution of results. The review should avoid filling that gap with assumptions about consistency or exceptional cases. Likewise, the memory figures identify a tradeoff without establishing whether it is acceptable for a particular operating environment. That judgment requires an operational context that is not supplied by the trial summary alone.

No mobile clients were included. The findings should therefore remain tied to the scope that was actually examined, with mobile behavior left as an open question. More broadly, the observed relationships are correlations rather than causal proof of what would happen in production. It would be premature to treat the trial as confirmation of a production benefit or to assume that the same balance of latency and memory would hold under conditions that were not tested.

Operational rollout has not been approved. The specified rollback condition is a failure rate above 2%, and that condition should remain explicit in any further review of the proposed change. A rollback rule does not itself authorize deployment or resolve the limits of the evidence. The next decision should weigh the measured latency and memory changes together, preserve the stated testing scope, and keep approval separate from the interpretation of these preliminary results.
