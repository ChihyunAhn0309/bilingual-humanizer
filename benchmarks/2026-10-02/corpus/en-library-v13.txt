The proposed community library pilot would keep the building open until 20:00 on Tuesday and Thursday evenings for 6 weeks. It should be clearly described as temporary. The pilot would not establish a permanent policy, and discussion of future hours should keep any permanent change separate from the pilot decision.

Of the 42 feedback forms received by the library, 28 support later hours, 9 prefer the current schedule, and 5 express no preference. These figures offer a useful starting point for review by showing how the submitted opinions are distributed, but they do not capture every library user's needs. The review should consider support for later hours alongside the reasons some respondents would retain the current schedule, without treating either group as representative of everyone.

The feedback expresses preferences; it does not show that those preferences led to visits. Nor does the available information establish that longer hours cause attendance to rise. The review should keep this limitation visible when weighing the proposal.

Volunteer availability remains unresolved and should not be left as a detail to settle after the pilot starts. A decision can acknowledge the interest in later hours while still requiring clarity about volunteer coverage. This is needed to assess whether the proposed evening schedule is workable.

Later opening would leave the quiet room's capacity at 12. That limit should inform descriptions of what the extended hours could offer, and communications about the proposal should distinguish the building's opening hours from the space available for quiet use.
