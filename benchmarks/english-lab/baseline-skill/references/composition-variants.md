# Compare meaningful complete compositions

Use when the user explicitly requests stronger detector-oriented humanization and ordinary feedback-led editing has reached a plateau. This is a bounded option, not a requirement to rewrite effective prose. Keep the existing total revision budget; compare at most two additional complete candidates, counting each within that budget. If no meaningful route exists, keep the current text.

## Plan before scoring

Read the source, completed detector review and latest native result. A low Human value does not locate a defect. Map the reader purpose, source-backed voice, protected propositions and paragraph functions. Propose genuinely different ways to fulfil the same purpose, such as leading a review with decision conditions before its evidence, following a personal account's supplied sequence, or organising a notice around the reader's next action.

Choose routes from the actual source. Do not impose these examples on unrelated genres. Preserve first-person experience only when supplied. Do not invent motives, feelings, causal transitions, sensory details, dialogue or incidental facts. Do not turn a formal obligation into casual advice or an unconfirmed situation into a conclusion.

State each route's proposed reader benefit before generating it. An alternative need not mean the original was defective. Do not promise that a detector will prefer a route. Compose from the meaning map and brief, then compare against the original; avoid making the previous draft the template. Keep information coverage without a word-count target.

## Review and select

Complete fidelity and reader reviews before scoring. Give an independent reviewer source and candidate without intended scores when authorized. Reject or repair semantic drift; a higher score cannot rescue it. Freeze each eligible complete text, then run the same detector and preserve native classes, scope and cautions. Do not tune the detector or use translation, random synonyms, typographical noise, fragment insertion or deletion of difficult passages.

The user's score objective may rank alternatives that already pass fidelity and reader-quality checks. Preserve an earlier checkpoint if alternatives are worse, unmeasured or fail review. Keep editorial selection and highest-scoring faithful text distinct when they trade off. Disclose unsuccessful alternatives and the exact final score; never combine favourable values from separately scored passages into a supposed whole-document result.

Try a promising route on a different source before presenting it as reusable improvement. A gain on one development text is not generic detector acceptance. Stop after the bounded comparison when no eligible gain is demonstrated. Do not increase the turn ceiling or turn the process into a synonym search.
