My warning system was a folded towel on the seat of a loose chair. The chair stayed in the corner of my kitchen for most of the winter. Visitors understood the warning, although I still explained the wobble every time someone came over. I had inherited the chair from my aunt; an awkward joint did not seem reason enough to throw it out.

Last Sunday I finally took it apart. The back came away with one careful pull, though I had expected the old glue to resist. There is a pale patch underneath that might be a repair or just where the finish wore off. I cannot remember whether my aunt ever repaired it herself. I left the patch alone.

The chair is usable now, though one leg still catches slightly on the uneven floor. I moved the towel back to its hook. The afternoon mostly gave me another place to sit. I would like to say it settled something about keeping my aunt's things, but the harder choices are still in the box above the wardrobe.
